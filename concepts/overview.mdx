An experience is one drop: the products on offer and everything about how they are released. It holds one or more sequences, and a sequence is one way in to an experience; each sequence runs distributions (a queue, draw, auction, timed release or appointment), one window at a time. Every customer who arrives is placed in exactly one sequence, and goes through that sequence's gates and its open distribution to the right to buy.

## How the pieces nest

| Layer | What it holds | Explained in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organisation | Your plan, your checkout, products, customers, audiences, brand and templates, locations and loyalty programme. | The table below |
| Experience | Up to 10 products, a time zone, its design and (on Growth and above) its hosted page, its announcements, and its sequences. | [Experiences](/concepts/experiences) |
| Sequence | An optional audience and an optional access code, its own share of the stock, how products are assigned (**Customer Picks** or **Mystery Box**), order limits, a waitlist, and a priority. | [Sequences and routing](/concepts/sequences) |
| Distribution | One of the five types, with an **Opens** and a **Closes** time. Windows inside one sequence run one after another. | [Distributions](/concepts/distributions/overview) |

The waitlist belongs to the sequence. It is not a distribution and sells nothing: see [Waitlists](/concepts/waitlists).

The **Create Experience** wizard builds an experience, one sequence and one distribution together. You add further sequences and distributions afterwards. See [Create an experience](/guide/create/experiences).

## A customer's path through it

This is the order a customer goes through. What each screen says is on [What your customers see](/guide/drop/what-customers-see).

1. Arrives. On your hosted page, on a page of your own site that carries the Fanfare widget, or on your Shopify product page (with the Fanfare app in Shopify). See [Brand, templates and designs](/concepts/design).
2. Is placed in one sequence. Fanfare skips sequences the customer can never enter. Of the rest, it prefers what is open over what is scheduled, then a sequence they can enter now over one behind a gate, then the lowest priority number. See [Sequences and routing](/concepts/sequences).
3. Meets that sequence's gates. Signing in (or continuing as a guest, where every open or scheduled distribution in the sequence allows guests and the sequence has no audience), entering an access code, and being in the audience. See [Sequences and routing](/concepts/sequences) and [Customers and identity](/concepts/customers).
4. Can join the sequence's waitlist, if signed in, to hear when it opens. Sign-ups are taken only while nothing in the sequence is live and either a window is still to come or the sequence has no distributions yet. See [Waitlists](/concepts/waitlists).
5. Takes part in the open distribution. They wait in a [queue](/concepts/distributions/queue), enter a [draw](/concepts/distributions/draw), bid in an [auction](/concepts/distributions/auction), get in at a [timed release](/concepts/distributions/timed-release), or book a slot at an [appointment](/concepts/distributions/appointment).
6. Gets an admission. An admission is the right to buy, with a time limit to pay: the payment window. An appointment books a slot instead and sells nothing. See [Admission and the payment window](/concepts/admission-and-payment).
7. Pays. They pay in the checkout your organisation uses: Fanfare checkout, your own store's checkout, or Shopify checkout. On Fanfare checkout, if a queue or draw held their card when they joined, Fanfare takes the payment and creates the order, so they do not check out. If the payment window runs out first, they lose the admission and in most cases their unit goes back on sale. See [Checkout options](/concepts/checkout-options) and [Admission and the payment window](/concepts/admission-and-payment).

## Status follows the clock

There is no launch step. A distribution's status comes from its window and the clock: not yet open, open, closed, or deactivated before its end. A sequence takes its status from its distributions and the experience from its sequences, shown as **Draft** (no distributions yet), **Scheduled**, **Live**, **Ended early** or **Ended**, so one live distribution makes the whole experience **Live**. See [Experiences](/concepts/experiences).

## Rules that surprise people

- Open beats scheduled, whatever the priority. Priority follows the order you create sequences in and cannot be changed in the admin. See [Sequences and routing](/concepts/sequences).
- Windows inside one sequence cannot overlap. To run two things at the same time, use two sequences. See [Distributions](/concepts/distributions/overview).
- A waitlist takes sign-ups only while nothing in its sequence is live and either a window is still to come or the sequence has no distributions yet, so customers cannot join it while a queue is running or after the last window ends. See [Waitlists](/concepts/waitlists).
- A product's stock management is chosen when you create it and never changes. See [Stock](/concepts/stock).
- A plan decides what you can create and change, never whether what you already built keeps running. See [Plans and what they unlock](/guide/start/plans).
- Deactivate cannot be undone. See [What locks, and when](/concepts/what-locks-when).

## Worked example

Northbound Supply Co. is the organisation. Its experience "Phantom Stitch Anorak Drop" sells the Phantom Stitch Anorak through two sequences:

| Sequence | Priority | Who can enter | Distribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inner Circle Early Access | P1 | Members of the "Inner Circle" audience | The queue "Inner Circle Queue" |
| Public Draw | P2 | Everyone | The draw "Public Draw", which opens after the queue |

Each sequence has its own waitlist and its own share of the anorak's units.

An Inner Circle member arrives while the queue is open. Inner Circle Early Access is open and comes first, so they are placed in it. They sign in, Fanfare finds them in the audience, and they join the queue. When they reach the front they get an admission and have their payment window to pay.

A signed-in customer who is not in the audience arrives at the same time. Fanfare skips Inner Circle Early Access for them without asking for anything, and places them in Public Draw. The draw has not opened yet, so they can join Public Draw's waitlist. When the draw opens they enter it, and if they are selected as a winner they get an admission of their own.

The full timeline of who is placed where, hour by hour, is on [Sequences and routing](/concepts/sequences).

## On Shopify

- The Fanfare app in Shopify never offers Appointment.
- Products come from your Shopify catalogue. See [Products from Shopify](/guide/shopify/products).
- Customers enter through the product widget on your product page and pay in Shopify checkout, and a checkout rule stops anyone buying a protected product without getting in. See [Checkout protection](/guide/shopify/checkout-protection).
- The model itself is the same: experience, sequences, distributions, routing and admission.
