An experience is the container for one drop. It holds a name, a time zone, up to 10 products, its design and, on Growth and above, its hosted page (see [Design](/concepts/design)), its announcements, and one or more sequences. You create one experience per drop, and everything you see about that drop in the Fanfare admin hangs off it. Who can get in, and how, is set on the sequences inside it, not on the experience: see [Sequences and routing](/concepts/sequences).

## What an experience holds

An experience contains sequences, and each sequence contains distributions:

- **Experience**: the products on offer, the time zone, the design and page, and the announcements.
- **Sequence**: one way in to the experience, with its own gates, its own share of the stock and a waitlist.- **Distribution**: the way customers get access inside a sequence: a queue, draw, auction, timed release or appointment. A sequence can run several, one after another.

When you finish **Experiences → Create Experience**, Fanfare builds all three in one go: the experience, one sequence with the same name as the experience, and one distribution. The wizard's first step, **Experience type**, picks only the type of that first distribution. You can add sequences and distributions of other types later, and the Experiences list's **Types** column shows every type an experience holds.

Appointment is available on Growth and above. The other four types are available on every plan. See [Distributions](/concepts/distributions/overview) and [Plans](/guide/start/plans).

## Status chips

Every experience and sequence shows one status chip: **Draft**, **Scheduled**, **Live**, **Ended early** or **Ended**.

A distribution's card shows its own status, which comes from its window: **Upcoming** (or "Opens" with the date) before it opens, **Live** while it is open, **Ended** after it closes, and **Ended early** when it was deactivated before its end. An auction's card shows its phase instead, such as **Bidding** or **Settled**. When each type's window opens and ends is explained in [Distributions](/concepts/distributions/overview).

A sequence takes its chip from its distributions, and the experience takes its chip from its sequences, in the same way, with a distribution that has not opened yet counting as **Scheduled**. The first of these that any child has wins:

| Order | Chip | When the experience shows it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Live** | At least one distribution is open. |
| 2 | **Scheduled** | Nothing is open, and at least one distribution has not opened yet. |
| 3 | **Ended early** | Nothing is open or still to come, and at least one distribution was deactivated before its end. |
| 4 | **Ended** | Every distribution has run to its end. |
| none | **Draft** | The experience has no distributions. |

So one live sequence makes the whole experience **Live**, even if every other sequence has finished. The chip is not what customers see: if the experience has a published hosted page, deactivating any one sequence or distribution makes that page show the whole experience as ended, including for sequences still running. See [What locks when](/concepts/what-locks-when). A deactivated distribution is never relabelled **Ended** because a sibling ran on schedule.

**Draft** only means there is no distribution yet. It does not mean unpublished. The wizard always creates a distribution, so a new experience starts at **Scheduled** or **Live**.

## There is no launch button

An experience does not wait to be published. It is **Scheduled** as soon as a distribution has a future opening time, and **Live** the moment that distribution opens. Selecting **Create experience** on the **Review & launch** step schedules the drop.

If you leave the wizard's opening time as it is, the drop opens almost as soon as you select **Create experience**: see [Create an experience](/guide/create/experiences).

The **Review & launch** step says "You can still tweak anything after publishing." That overstates it: not everything can be changed afterwards, and deactivating cannot be undone. See [What locks when](/concepts/what-locks-when).

## Opens and Closes are worked out, not set

The experience's **Opens** and **Closes** are not fields you fill in. Fanfare works them out from the distributions:

- **Opens** is the earliest opening time among its distributions.
- **Closes** is the latest close among them. If a distribution has an opening time but no end time and has not been deactivated, **Closes** shows a dash.
- Deactivating brings **Closes** forward: a distribution that was running is counted as closing at the moment it was deactivated, and one deactivated before it opened is left out.

To move them, change the distributions' windows. Announcements timed relative to the experience's opening or close move when **Opens** or **Closes** move.

The Experiences list's strip "Live now and opening in the next 14 days" lists experiences whose **Opens** has passed and whose **Closes** has not, and those whose **Opens** is in the next 14 days. Its cards read **Opens** and **Closes**, so in a gap between sequences a card can show **Live** while the experience's chip shows **Scheduled**.

## Read an experience at a glance

Open an experience to see where it stands. The header shows:

- the status chip
- "Sequences: N" with a count per chip, for example "Sequences: 2 (1 Live | 1 Scheduled)"
- **Opens** and **Closes**, in the experience's time zone
- **Deactivated** with the date of the latest deactivation, once everything has stopped and anything in the experience was deactivated

The **Overview** tab starts with the "What happens next" card:

- the next moment, such as "Queue opens" or "Winners drawn", with a countdown to it
- one lane per sequence, listing its moments in order, with who the sequence is for and, except for appointment-only sequences, its waitlist
- "Times in" and the experience's time zone, with a **Show in my time** switch when your browser's time zone differs from the experience's
- which products it sells, and who counts the stock, for example "Fanfare counts stock", "Shopify counts stock" or "Counted per sequence: each sequence stops selling at its own limits". Stock is explained in [Stock](/concepts/stock).

When nothing is left to happen, the card says "Everything scheduled has happened." When nothing has a time yet, it says "Nothing is scheduled yet. Add a distribution to a sequence to give it a time."

## Time zone

Each experience has its own time zone. The Experiences list, the header and the "What happens next" card show times in that zone.

Customers see the widget's "Opens" time in their own time zone, and countdowns run to the same moment wherever they are. A date on the hosted page taken from the **Experience opening time** or **Experience closing time** shows in the experience's zone. See [What your customers see](/guide/drop/what-customers-see).

You can change the time zone later, but the change behaves differently depending on whether anything in the experience has opened yet. See [Create an experience](/guide/create/experiences).

## How an experience ends

An experience reads **Ended** once every distribution's window has closed. It reads **Ended early** once everything has stopped and at least one distribution was deactivated before its end. Deactivate and Delete are both one-way: what each does is explained in [What locks when](/concepts/what-locks-when), and the steps are in [Stop a drop](/guide/drop/deactivate).

Deleting an experience keeps its orders. An old link to a deleted experience opens a page saying it was deleted, with "It was deleted on" and the date, and "Its orders are still in Orders."

## Worked example: a members' queue, then a public draw

Northbound Supply Co. runs "Phantom Stitch Anorak Drop" with two sequences: "Inner Circle Early Access", running a queue that is open now and closes tomorrow, and "Public Draw", running a draw that opens in two days. Here is what the header chip and the "What happens next" card show as time passes.

| When | Inner Circle Early Access | Public Draw | Experience chip | Next moment on the card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Now, while the queue runs | **Live** | **Scheduled** | **Live** | "Queue closes", in the Inner Circle Early Access lane |
| After the queue closes, before the draw opens | **Ended** | **Scheduled** | **Scheduled** | "Entries open", in the Public Draw lane |
| While the draw takes entries | **Ended** | **Live** | **Live** | The draw's next moment, such as "Winners drawn" |
| After both have closed | **Ended** | **Ended** | **Ended** | "Everything scheduled has happened." |
| Experience deactivated while the queue runs | **Ended early** | **Ended early** | **Ended early** | Deactivated distributions drop off the card. With nothing left, it can read "Nothing is scheduled yet." |

Announcements and waitlist emails are moments too, so the card shows one of those first if it comes sooner.

Deactivating in the gap between the two sequences would also read **Ended early**, because the draw was stopped before it opened, while the queue's card stays **Ended**.

## On Shopify

- The Fanfare app in Shopify never offers Appointment. Its **Experience type** step and the empty Experiences list offer Queue, Draw, Auction and Timed Release.
- You can start an experience from a product in Shopify admin: the Fanfare experiences block on the product page has a **Start a Fanfare experience** button when the product has none.
- That block labels each experience **Active**, **Upcoming** or **Closed**, not with the status chips above.
- Saving an experience can report that Shopify gating could not be updated for some products, and deleting one that it could not be removed.

All three are covered in [Fanfare inside Shopify admin](/guide/shopify/in-shopify-admin).
